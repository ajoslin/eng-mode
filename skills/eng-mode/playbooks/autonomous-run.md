### Autonomous run

**You own the exit condition. Define done, then drive to it without stopping.** For "going to bed", "run until done", or sustained work against one predicate.

1. Before iteration one, state one checkable exit predicate and its real verification surface: for example, `the failing repro passes and its regression test is green`, `all N named PRs are merged`, or `pixel diff = 0 on the mapped verify-project flow`. Record scope, human gates, terminal evidence, and a bounded loop limit. A vague goal is not a predicate. Invoke `goal` with that predicate and keep the finite execution graph in `todo`; the lead owns both.
2. Pick the wake mechanism. When CI, a merge, a ref advance, a process, or another agent exposes an event, arm its blocking watcher and use `hub` completion or a message to wake the lead; retain a bounded, long heartbeat as crash/lost-event fallback. When no event exists, invoke `loop` with a fixed heartbeat sized to when new evidence is worth checking and the recorded iteration or time limit. Do not poll faster than the source can change.
3. Each iteration re-observes current reality, then executes one smallest verifiable unit under the **sequence-verifiable-units** principle: make the evidence-backed change, verify it on the predicate's surface, and do not begin the next unit until that check completes. Commit only when the predicate advances. Revert speculative changes and non-wins in full; never leave a guard or belt-and-suspenders change that merely might help. Never edit the ruler, workload, baseline, or other predicate inputs to manufacture movement.
4. Recoverable failures do not stop the run. Repair broken skills, related bugs, flaky verifiers, review noise or failures, tool failures, fixable drift, and orphaned follow-ups; re-arm a lost watcher and resume from the last committed advancing unit after a crash. Isolate a required out-of-band fix in its own task-owned commit or, when PR delivery is already authorized, its own PR. Park a nonblocking side fix separately with an owner and resumption condition rather than widening the run. Then return to the predicate. Do not turn reversible work into a human gate.
5. Append exactly one `show-me-your-work` trail row every iteration: iteration id, observation, unit attempted, verification evidence, predicate before/after, kept commit or reverted work, side-fix disposition, and next wake. The trail and advancing commits are the restart source of truth; duplicate no state.
6. After every verification, evaluate the predicate, plateau, and loop limit. A plateau means pivot: change hypothesis or mechanism, re-instrument, or revisit evidence; it is not a stop. Never relax the predicate. Stop active work only before a destructive action, for a genuine product or preference choice no experiment can settle, or at a proven dead end after plausible mechanisms and evidence sources are exhausted.
7. End in exactly one terminal state and make it durable:
   - `completed`: the predicate and verification contract pass; record the evidence, stop `loop`, then complete `goal`.
   - `loop-limit`: the bounded limit fired with the predicate unmet; `loop` is stopped, `goal` remains incomplete, and the trail names the next evidence-backed pivot. This is not success or a dead end.
   - `paused`: a destructive action or unreachable product choice blocks progress; checkpoint via Pause safely, stop or pause `loop`, and leave `goal` incomplete with the exact gate and resume condition.
   - `dead-end`: evidence shows the remaining plausible paths cannot satisfy the unchanged predicate; checkpoint the exhausted paths and disconfirming evidence, stop `loop`, and leave `goal` incomplete.

**Reply:** the objective and exit predicate; iterations run; what landed and what was discarded; side fixes repaired or parked; final predicate evidence and state; terminal state (`completed`, `loop-limit`, `paused`, or `dead-end`); final `goal` state; and final `loop` state.
